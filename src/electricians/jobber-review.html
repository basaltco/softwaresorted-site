Quick Verdict

Jobber is a polished product. The mobile app is genuinely good, the client portal makes a small firm look like a bigger one, and the offline mode added in January 2026 fixed one of its biggest weaknesses. The everyday loop — quote, schedule, invoice, get paid — runs smoothly.

The problem for a UK electrician is that it wasn't built for you. It's a Canadian product priced in US dollars, it has no native CIS handling or EICR certificates, and Jobber's own help centre documents QuickBooks sync limitations. If you're a two-person team whose accountant uses Xero, Tradify does the core job for less. If you're three or more people, care more about how clients experience you than about compliance paperwork, and don't need certificates, Jobber earns its place.

What Jobber Actually Does

Where Jobber pulls ahead is the client side. Your customers get their own portal to approve quotes, check job history, pay invoices and ask for new work — without ringing you. If your clients are property managers or facilities companies who expect a professional experience, that's worth real money. Underneath, it covers the full job lifecycle you'd expect: online booking, quoting, scheduling, crew dispatch, time tracking, invoicing and payment collection.

The scheduling calendar is strong — drag-and-drop, crew availability view, and route optimisation that adjusts in real time as the day changes. Assigning jobs to the nearest available engineer is arguably easier here than in Tradify or Powered Now.

Then there are the AI tools. The Marketing Suite writes client follow-up campaigns and review requests from a short prompt, and the AI Receptionist answers website enquiries and books jobs automatically — handy if you're a sole trader missing leads while on-site. Jobber now groups both under the "Jobber Copilot" name, though they're still separate products, and both cost extra below the Plus plan (see pricing).

Offline mode arrived in January 2026. Jobs, notes and photos save on the phone and sync when you're back in signal. It works — but it's new, and Powered Now's offline mode is the more established of the two.

Two things to get your head round first. Jobber prices everything in US dollars — there's no native GBP pricing, even on its UK-facing site — so what you pay in pounds depends on your card provider's exchange rate at the time of billing. And each plan has three prices: cheapest if you prepay a year, more on a 12-month commitment billed monthly, most if you go month to month with no commitment. Additional users cost $29/mo on any plan. 14-day free trial, no card required.

The number to watch is the jump from one person to two. Core looks competitive at $29/mo (prepaid annual), but add a second person and you're on Connect at $99/mo (prepaid annual) — more than triple. For a two-person electrical team, Tradify at £68/mo is considerably cheaper. From three people, though, Connect's flat price works out cheaper than Tradify's per-user rate at current exchange rates (£1 ≈ $1.34, checked 2026-10-06).

And the subscription is the floor, not the ceiling. UK card payments via Jobber Payments cost 1.4% + 20p per transaction (£0.50 minimum transaction) — notably better than the 2.9% + $0.30 US rate often quoted for this platform. The Marketing Suite is $79/mo on plans below Plus, and the AI Receptionist $99/mo unless you're on Plus.

How It Works on a Real Job

A facilities manager emails about an EICR on a commercial unit. You create the job in Jobber, assign it to the engineer's calendar, and the client automatically receives a booking confirmation with a link to their client hub portal. The engineer opens Jobber on-site — offline if needed — views all job details, logs time, takes and annotates photos. When the job is done, the client portal sends the invoice automatically. They pay online. Payment clears the same day via Jobber Payments.

That's a smooth workflow — right up to the point where you need the EICR itself. Jobber doesn't have electrical compliance certificates, so you're back to paper or a separate tool for the document the job was booked for. If you mostly do domestic work, you'll rarely notice. If compliance work is a big part of your week, it's a real missing piece, and you'll feel it on every certificate job.

The Catch

QuickBooks sync has documented limitations. Jobber's own help centre documents QuickBooks Online sync limitations: custom one-off line items sync as a generic "Custom Service" item with the name moved into the description, and items customised in QuickBooks (sub-items, different income accounts) cause sync errors that stop the invoice syncing. If your accountant relies on QuickBooks for clean automatic reconciliation, test this thoroughly during the trial.

Sharp pricing jump at team size. Core to Connect goes from $29/mo to $99/mo (both prepaid annual) the moment you add one employee. At two people Tradify's per-user model is cheaper; from three people, Connect overtakes at current exchange rates.

No CIS, no UK compliance certificates. It's a Canadian product, and this is where it shows. CIS reverse charge invoicing, EICR certificates, MTD — all need workarounds or separate tools. For domestic-only electricians that may not matter; for subcontract or certification work it's a meaningful gap.

Google Calendar only syncs about twice a day. Jobber's own help centre says it syncs to Google Calendar roughly every 12 hours, and Google offers no manual refresh — so a job booked this morning may not show up in an engineer's Google Calendar until much later in the day. If your team needs faster updates, Jobber points to Apple Calendar, which syncs as often as every 5 minutes.

Watch the price. One long-term US user on Trustpilot (Aug 2026) wrote that Jobber's "reports options are extremely limited" and that "they KEEP raising the price." Check current pricing against your team size before committing.

How It Compares

Against Tradify: Tradify is the cheaper option for small teams, integrates with Xero, and is built with UK trades in mind; Jobber is the stronger scheduler, with richer client automation and more AI features. One or two people on a budget and using Xero, Tradify. Three or more, wanting a polished client experience and no need for UK compliance features, Jobber is competitive — see the full comparison page.

Against Powered Now: these are built for different jobs. Powered Now is the compliance tool — CIS, EICR certificates, MTD and offline working, all native. Jobber is the client-experience tool — scheduling sophistication, client portal, AI features. Work out which of those is your bigger headache, and the choice makes itself.

The Break-Even Maths

On paper the maths is easy: at Core ($29/mo prepaid annual), a sole trader needs to save roughly half an hour of admin a week at £55/hr to break even. The harder question is whether it's the right $29. At current exchange rates it's actually cheaper than Tradify Lite (£34/mo ≈ $45.50), but Tradify is built with UK trades in mind and integrates with Xero — so for a sole trader the deciding factor is less the price than whether you'll use the client portal, AI marketing and scheduling optimisation.

Who This Is NOT For

Teams of 2–4 on a budget. The Core to Connect jump makes Jobber expensive for small crews. Tradify's per-user model is cheaper at that team size.

Anyone whose accountant relies on QuickBooks auto-reconciliation. Jobber documents the sync limitations in its help centre. Test it thoroughly during the trial and verify with your accountant before switching.

Electricians who regularly produce compliance certificates. EICRs, Minor Works, Electrical Installation Certificates — not in Jobber. Powered Now is the answer.

Subcontractors working under CIS with VAT-registered clients. No native reverse charge invoicing. Manual workarounds needed every time.
