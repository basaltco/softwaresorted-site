Quick Verdict

ServiceM8 is the tool that makes a small electrical firm look organised to its customers. It's clean and fast, and its automated client messages — the "on my way" texts, arrival windows and completion summaries — are the reason to choose it; for businesses doing high volumes of domestic work they're arguably worth the price on their own. But if you're assessing it for a UK electrical team, there's one thing to test before you commit: the Android app. ServiceM8 says its Android app (ServiceM8 Lite) has "almost all the same functionality as the iOS app", including tracking time on site and clocking on and off. The gaps it lists are room scanning, drawing diagrams, editing PDFs and in-camera photo tagging. Reviewers report the Android experience as weaker in practice, and one Capterra reviewer said their business had to change from Android to iPhone at the same time as adopting ServiceM8. If any of your engineers are on Android, test it on their phones during the free trial.

If your team is on iPhone, ServiceM8 is hard to beat at this price. If you have Android users and the trial shows the Android app falls short for them, Tradify is the more practical choice.

What ServiceM8 Actually Does

ServiceM8 is an Australian-built field service platform that covers the full job lifecycle: online booking, quoting, job assignment, scheduling, real-time field updates, invoicing and payment collection. None of that is unusual. What sets it apart is the automation on top. It can text a client "on my way" when your engineer leaves the depot, send a completion summary when the work is done, and follow up at intervals you choose — all without anyone in the office lifting a finger. That's arguably more sophisticated client communication than Tradify or Powered Now currently offer, and for a domestic electrician it means fewer no-shows and fewer "where's the electrician?" calls.

The custom forms builder is the other headline feature. You can build detailed site documentation forms — risk assessments, job-specific checklists, compliance sign-offs — from scratch using a drag-and-drop interface. If you need to document site conditions or client sign-offs beyond standard certificates, it's arguably more flexible than Tradify's basic forms. Assets management lets you track installed equipment at each client site — useful for maintenance contracts where you need a record of what's been fitted and when it was last serviced.

The scheduling board uses a drag-and-drop calendar with real-time engineer location visibility, colour-coded job statuses, and the ability to reassign jobs instantly when plans change. Offline, engineers can view and update jobs already synced to the phone and save notes and photos; final PDF quotes, invoices and Forms, and emails and texts, wait for signal. That's a degree of offline resilience, though not the same as Powered Now's full offline mode.

Pricing is per account, not per user — meaning unlimited staff can be added on any paid plan, which compares favourably to Tradify's per-user model for larger teams. Jobs are counted per completed job, not per user. The catch is that a busy month costs more than a quiet one, which makes forward budgeting harder. For a sole trader the per-job model gets expensive at volume — 200 jobs a month means the £119/mo Premium plan, against £34/mo for one Tradify Lite user. For teams it tilts the other way, because ServiceM8 doesn't charge per user.

Additional costs to factor in: paid plans include an SMS allowance (from 100 a month on Starter) with extra messages at 5p each, and the ServiceM8 Phone system is an add-on from £15/mo. Storage is unlimited on every plan. The 14-day free trial gives full access to all features — enough time to run real jobs through it and assess fit.

How It Works on a Real Job

A property management company calls to book an emergency callout — a distribution board issue at a commercial unit. Here's how ServiceM8 handles it from that call to payment:

The call comes through ServiceM8's phone integration, which logs it automatically and creates a new job record. You assign it to your nearest available engineer on the scheduling board, who receives an instant notification on their iPhone with the job address, client details, and any site notes. ServiceM8 automatically sends the client a text confirming the booking and estimated arrival window. When your engineer leaves the depot, another automated text goes to the client: "Your engineer James is on his way, estimated arrival 2:15pm." The client gets GPS tracking in some configurations.

On-site, the engineer opens the job on their iPhone, views all the details, logs time, takes photos, and fills in any custom forms you've set up for this job type. When the work is done, the client signs off digitally on the engineer's phone. ServiceM8 generates the invoice automatically from the completed job record, the client receives it by email, and they can pay by card via the integrated payment link. The whole job — from initial call to payment — is documented in one place without a single piece of paper.

That workflow is where ServiceM8 earns its reputation. The automation is properly impressive for a tool at this price point. The gap for UK electricians is the compliance layer: ServiceM8 has no built-in EICR, Minor Works or other electrical certificates, and handles the domestic reverse charge only through a one-time setup (0% tax rates and a separate invoice template) — it doesn't document CIS deductions. Some paid third-party forms, such as a Minor Works certificate, are sold separately through ServiceM8 partners; for anything else you'll need a separate system or manual process.

The Catch

Android users report a weaker experience. ServiceM8 says its Android app (ServiceM8 Lite) has "almost all the same functionality as the iOS app", including tracking time on site and clocking on and off. The gaps it lists are room scanning, drawing diagrams, editing PDFs and in-camera photo tagging. Reviewers tell a harsher story. One Capterra reviewer said their business had to change from Android to iPhone at the same time as adopting ServiceM8. A UK Trustpilot reviewer who moved from Apple to Android wrote that "The android app has barely any functions that the apple one does to the point where its pretty pointless" (Chris Harvey, Mar 2026). If any of your engineers are on Android, test it on their phones during the free trial.

Per-job pricing becomes expensive at volume. At 200+ jobs a month the monthly cost climbs significantly, and for a sole trader Tradify at £34/mo works out cheaper at that volume. Budget carefully if your job count varies seasonally — a busy summer month could cost materially more than a quiet winter one.

No built-in UK compliance certificates. EICRs, Minor Works, and Electrical Installation Certificates aren't part of ServiceM8. Some paid third-party forms, such as a Minor Works certificate, are sold separately through ServiceM8 partners. The UK domestic reverse charge needs a one-time setup — 0% reverse charge tax rates and a separate invoice template carrying the required note — and CIS deductions aren't documented. For compliance-heavy electrical work, this is a meaningful gap.

Recurring jobs have a scheduling quirk. One Software Advice reviewer has noted that recurring jobs are calculated on 24-hour periods rather than working hours, which causes them to appear incorrectly in the calendar when a job spans multiple consecutive days. The display confuses both field engineers and clients. It's a minor but persistent bug.

How It Compares

Against Tradify: ServiceM8's client automation and custom forms are more powerful; Tradify's identical Android app and flat per-user pricing are the more practical choice for a mixed-device team. So it comes down to your phones. All on iPhone and keen on automation — ServiceM8 is worth serious consideration. Anyone on Android — Tradify is the lower-risk pick.

Against Powered Now: ServiceM8 wins on automation and forms flexibility. Powered Now wins comprehensively on UK compliance — CIS, MTD, and native electrical certificates. For electricians who regularly produce EICRs or work as subcontractors under CIS, Powered Now addresses requirements that ServiceM8 simply doesn't support natively.

The Break-Even Maths

On the Starter plan at £25/mo for 50 jobs: roughly 27 minutes of admin saved per month (about 6 minutes a week) at a £55/hr rate to break even. The automated client communications alone — no-show reduction from "on my way" texts, faster payment from automated invoice follow-ups — realistically cover that. One Trustpilot reviewer reported saving 20–30 hours of admin per week — an exceptional result, but it shows the ceiling.

The maths shifts at higher volumes. At 200 jobs per month, you're on the Premium plan at £119/mo. At that point the break-even is a little over two hours of admin saved per month (roughly half an hour a week) — still achievable, but worth verifying against your actual job complexity rather than assuming.

Who This Is NOT For

Teams with Android users who haven't tested it. ServiceM8 says its Android app has almost all the iPhone app's functionality, but reviewers report it as weaker in practice. Don't assume either way — run real jobs on your engineers' Android phones during the free trial before committing.

Electricians who regularly produce compliance certificates. EICRs, Minor Works, Electrical Installation Certificates — none are built into ServiceM8. You'll need Powered Now, Tradify Pro, or paid third-party forms alongside it.

Subcontractors working under CIS with VAT-registered clients. ServiceM8 handles the reverse charge through a one-time template setup, but doesn't document CIS deductions. Powered Now documents built-in CIS and reverse charge support.

High-volume operations where job count varies significantly month to month. The per-job pricing model makes cashflow forecasting less predictable. Tradify's flat rate is easier to budget around.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does ServiceM8 work on Android?

Yes, through its Android app, ServiceM8 Lite. ServiceM8 says its Android app (ServiceM8 Lite) has "almost all the same functionality as the iOS app", including tracking time on site and clocking on and off. The gaps it lists are room scanning, drawing diagrams, editing PDFs and in-camera photo tagging. Reviewers report the Android experience as weaker in practice, so test it on your engineers' phones during the free trial before committing. One Capterra reviewer said their business had to change from Android to iPhone at the same time as adopting ServiceM8.

How does ServiceM8 pricing work?

ServiceM8 charges per job completed per month, not per user. There's a free plan for one user and 30 jobs a month; paid plans start at £25/mo for 50 jobs and scale with volume, with unlimited users. For a sole trader it's cheap at low volumes but gets expensive as job count climbs; for teams, not paying per user often makes it cheaper than per-user tools. A busy month costs more than a quiet one, which makes forward budgeting harder.

Does ServiceM8 handle CIS or EICR certificates?

No. ServiceM8 is an Australian product and UK compliance features aren't built in: EICR certificates need paid third-party forms, the domestic reverse charge needs a one-time template setup, and CIS deductions aren't documented. For domestic-only work this rarely matters. For any compliance-heavy or subcontractor work, Powered Now is the appropriate tool.

Is ServiceM8 good for UK electricians?

For iPhone-based teams doing high volumes of domestic work, yes — the client automation and custom forms are a real strength. For compliance certificate requirements or CIS work, it's the wrong tool, and teams with Android users should test the Android app during the trial first. For those, Tradify or Powered Now will suit you better.
