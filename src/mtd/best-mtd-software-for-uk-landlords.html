If you're a UK landlord with qualifying property income over £50,000, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax stopped being a future problem on 6 April 2026 — it's now a live legal requirement. You need to keep digital records of your rental income and expenses and send HMRC quarterly updates, rather than filing one Self Assessment return a year. The next threshold — £30,000 — brings a much larger group of landlords into scope from 6 April 2027.

This guide compares the four MTD-compatible tools SoftwareSorted has a working relationship with — FreeAgent, Xero, QuickBooks, and Sage — specifically for how well each one handles property income, not just general bookkeeping. That distinction matters more than it looks: not all "MTD-ready" accounting software treats a landlord's rental income the same way it treats a sole trader's business income.

Quick Verdict

FreeAgent and Sage both offer accounts built specifically around property income, with per-property profit tracking — the more natural fit if managing your portfolio clearly, not just filing compliantly, matters to you. Xero sits in the middle: solid MTD-for-landlords support with its own dedicated hub, but less property-specific structure than FreeAgent or Sage. QuickBooks is MTD-compliant and the cheapest entry point, but it has no dedicated property module — tracking multiple properties relies on its Classes/Locations feature (higher tiers only), and combining rental income with a separate self-employment income stream requires two separate QuickBooks accounts rather than one.

If you manage one or two properties and want the simplest MTD-compliant option: Xero's Simple tier or QuickBooks are both workable. If you manage a multi-property portfolio and want to see profitability per property: FreeAgent or Sage are the better starting point.

Why This Decision Matters Now

Phase 1 of MTD for Income Tax Self Assessment applies to sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 in the 2024–25 tax year, and it has been mandatory since 6 April 2026. Phase 2 drops the threshold to £30,000 from 6 April 2027, and Phase 3 drops it again to £20,000 from 6 April 2028 — so the population required to comply gets significantly larger every year through the rest of the decade.

A genuine exemption exists, but HMRC has been explicit about what does and doesn't qualify: age, disability, remote location, or a religious objection to using computers can exempt you. "I'm not comfortable with software," "I don't have many transactions," and "it costs money" are explicitly not accepted grounds. If you're a landlord above the threshold, digital record-keeping and quarterly updates are the default expectation, not an option to weigh up.

Worth knowing: MTD ITSA applies to individuals — sole traders and landlords — not limited companies. If you hold property through a limited company, this mandate doesn't apply to you; you're already filing under Corporation Tax rules. This guide is for landlords who hold property personally or in a partnership, not through a company structure.

Who This Is NOT For

This guide is for individual UK landlords and partnerships with qualifying property income who are in scope for MTD ITSA now or in the next phase. It is not for:

Limited companies holding property — MTD ITSA applies to individuals, not companies. If you hold property through a limited company, you're already under Corporation Tax rules and this mandate doesn't apply to you.

Landlords with qualifying income below £20,000 — you are not yet in scope under any confirmed phase (Phase 3, from 6 April 2028, brings the threshold to £20,000; nothing below that has been confirmed as mandatory).

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need MTD software if I'm a landlord earning under £50,000?

Not yet, but the threshold is falling. Phase 2 brings landlords with qualifying income over £30,000 into scope from 6 April 2027, and Phase 3 brings the £20,000 threshold from 6 April 2028. If you're close to a threshold, starting digital record-keeping ahead of the mandatory date isn't required, but it removes the scramble when your phase begins.

Which software is best for a landlord with more than one property?

FreeAgent and Sage both offer purpose-built per-property tracking, letting you see profitability property-by-property rather than as one combined total. QuickBooks can technically track multiple properties using its Classes/Locations feature, but that's only available on higher-priced tiers and isn't a dedicated property module.

Can I use the same software for rental income and self-employment income?

It depends on the tool, and this changes as vendors update their products — QuickBooks' own support documentation describes combined rental and self-employment income as needing two separate accounts. FreeAgent's landlord account is built to combine income types more directly, but confirm the current handling with the vendor before assuming it fits your exact situation.

Is MTD for Income Tax the same as MTD for VAT?

No. MTD for VAT has applied to VAT-registered businesses above the VAT threshold since 2022 and is a separate, already-established requirement. MTD for Income Tax Self Assessment (MTD ITSA) is the newer mandate covering sole traders' and landlords' income tax, being phased in from 6 April 2026 by income threshold. This guide covers MTD ITSA for landlords specifically.

What happens if I don't comply once I'm in scope?

HMRC will expect digital records and quarterly updates once your qualifying income crosses your phase's threshold. This guide doesn't cover HMRC's penalty framework in detail — check current guidance directly on gov.uk or with an accountant, since exact penalty mechanics for MTD ITSA are a compliance question this page isn't positioned to answer for your specific situation.
