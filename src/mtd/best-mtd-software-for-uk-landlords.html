If you're a UK landlord with qualifying income over £50,000 in 2024–25, Making Tax Digital for Income Tax stopped being a future problem on 6 April 2026. It's now a legal requirement. Qualifying income is your total gross income from self-employment and property combined, before expenses — not your rent alone. HMRC's own example: £25,000 of rental income plus £27,000 of self-employment income is £52,000 of qualifying income. Once you're in, you keep digital records, send HMRC quarterly updates, and then submit your tax return through the same software by 31 January after the tax year ends. The next threshold — over £30,000 in 2025–26 — brings a much larger group of landlords into scope from 6 April 2027.

This guide compares four MTD-compatible tools — FreeAgent, Xero, QuickBooks, and Sage — specifically for how well each one handles property income, not just general bookkeeping. That distinction matters more than it looks: not all "MTD-ready" accounting software treats a landlord's rental income the same way it treats a sole trader's business income.

This page is editorial analysis of software options, not tax advice. Your MTD obligations depend on your own circumstances — verify them with HMRC directly or speak to a tax adviser before choosing software.

The catch: if you also have self-employment income, you'll need a second FreeAgent account. Xero says it can handle self-employment and property income in the same subscription, and it's the one accountants are most likely to know. Per-property tracking uses its general-purpose tracking categories rather than a property module. It's also the only one of the four that HMRC's software finder lists for foreign property income. Sage is the cheapest start: Sage Sole Trader is free for straightforward UK lets if you're not VAT-registered, its £7/month version can hold more than one income source, and VAT-registered landlords need Sage Accounting. QuickBooks works for a single, simple let, but it's the weakest landlord fit here — no property module, a separate account for each income source, and no support yet for overseas property under MTD.

One or two straightforward UK lets and not VAT-registered: start with Sage Sole Trader (free) or Xero Simple (£7/month). Several properties and you want profit per property: FreeAgent. Rental income plus self-employment income: Xero, or Sage Sole Trader's £7/month version, keeps both in one subscription — FreeAgent and QuickBooks need two accounts. Why This Decision Matters Now

Phase 1 of MTD for Income Tax applies to sole traders and landlords with qualifying income over £50,000 in the 2024–25 tax year, and it has been mandatory since 6 April 2026. Phase 2 brings in qualifying income over £30,000 in 2025–26, from 6 April 2027. Phase 3 brings in qualifying income over £20,000 in 2026–27, from 6 April 2028. At £20,000 or less you don't need to use it — the government has said it's exploring lower thresholds, but nothing is confirmed. If you have rental income and self-employment income, both count towards your qualifying income. If you own a property jointly, only your share of the property income counts — for example, an equal share of £50,000 of property income is £25,000. (HMRC's eligibility guidance sets out the rules.)

You can apply for an exemption if you're digitally excluded. HMRC's exemption guidance lists these grounds: your age, a health condition or a disability stops you using a computer, tablet or smartphone; you belong to a religious society whose beliefs are incompatible with digital communications; or you can't get internet access because of where you live. These reasons are not accepted on their own: you've filed a paper return before, you're unfamiliar with accountancy software, you only have a small number of digital records, or it will take extra time or cost to sign up and use software. HMRC considers other reasons case by case. If you're above the threshold and not exempt, digital records, quarterly updates and a year-end return through your software are the default, not an option to weigh up.

Worth knowing: MTD for Income Tax applies to individuals — sole traders and landlords — not limited companies. If you hold property through a limited company, it doesn't apply to you. Partnerships don't currently need to use it either — HMRC says they'll follow at a later date — and a partnership profit share doesn't count towards your qualifying income. This guide is for landlords who hold property personally.

The Four Tools Compared

Clearest per-property view

FreeAgent — dedicated landlord account type

Approx. pricing: Landlord plan from £10/month (list price; introductory promotional pricing is common — check freeagent.com for the current rate)

FreeAgent runs a separate UK Unincorporated Landlord account type with a Property feature. You attribute income and costs to individual properties and get a Profit & Loss report for each one — the only dedicated property module of the four tools here. The property features only exist in landlord accounts, and they're for UK property, not property overseas. Worth knowing: FreeAgent launched landlord accounts for accountants' clients in 2022 and opened them to all landlords in November 2023, so older reviews that call it unsuitable for landlords are out of date.

Catch: If you have both rental income and self-employment income, you'll need two FreeAgent accounts — a sole trader account and a landlord account. FreeAgent says property income can't be recorded in a sole trader account. Each account sends the quarterly updates for its own income stream. FreeAgent's help centre says you then make one final declaration, from either account — confirm the current process with FreeAgent before you set up.

Mixed income in one subscription

Xero — self-employment and property in one subscription

Approx. pricing: Simple tier from £7/month (only if you're not VAT-registered, and up to 10 invoices), Ignite tier from £18/month (list prices; check xero.com/uk for current promotional rates)

Xero publishes a dedicated Making Tax Digital for landlords hub and is listed in HMRC's software finder for sole trader, UK property and foreign property income. Xero also says you can manage your self-employment and property income in the same subscription — useful if you have both, though each income stream still sends its own quarterly updates. Check with Xero which plan you need for that. In our experience it's the one UK accountants are most likely to already know, which matters if you use one — familiarity means less friction at handover and filing time.

Catch: Xero has no property module. For per-property figures, Xero's own guides point to tracking categories — a general-purpose feature where you set up each property yourself. It works, but it's a setup job rather than a built-in property feature like FreeAgent's. We haven't confirmed which Xero plans include tracking categories, and Simple is capped at 10 invoices, so check the plan against your needs with Xero before choosing on price.

Weakest landlord-specific workflow

QuickBooks — MTD-compliant, no dedicated property module

Approx. pricing: Sole Trader Plus plan from £10/month (list price; QuickBooks runs aggressive introductory discounts — e.g. promotional rates as low as £1/month for a limited period — check quickbooks.intuit.com/uk for the current offer and the price it reverts to)

QuickBooks says it's ready for MTD for Income Tax, including UK property income. Its Sole Trader Plus plan is £10/month, the same as FreeAgent's Landlord plan. If your situation is simple — one UK property, no separate self-employment income — it will do the job.

Catch: QuickBooks has no dedicated property module. For per-property figures there are two general-purpose routes: Classes or Locations, only on Plus (£56/month) and Advanced, or — according to QuickBooks' help — tags with a profit and loss by tag report on Simple Start (£16/month) and above — check this with QuickBooks before signing up. Classes and Locations aren't on the £10 Sole Trader Plus plan, and we couldn't confirm tags are either. If you have both rental income and self-employment income, QuickBooks says each account can only connect to one primary income source for MTD, so you'd need two separate QuickBooks accounts. And QuickBooks doesn't currently support foreign property businesses for MTD, so it's not an option if you let property overseas.

Cheapest start for straightforward lets

Sage — free Sole Trader plan for straightforward UK lets

Approx. pricing: Sage Sole Trader free (or £7/month for the paid version); Sage Accounting Start £20/month, Standard £43/month, Plus £59/month (list prices; check sage.com/en-gb for current promotional rates)

Sage Sole Trader covers UK property income and is free if you're not VAT-registered — the lowest entry price of the four. The free version has one bank connection and one income source. The £7/month version allows multiple bank connections and can hold more than one income source — rental and self-employment, say — as separate businesses under one login and one subscription, each sending its own quarterly updates. Sage says Sole Trader suits landlords with straightforward needs.

Catch: Sage Sole Trader applies one ownership share across all your UK property income, so it can't handle different shares in different properties. There's no dedicated property module either: for a per-property view, Sage's help points to Analysis types, a general-purpose feature on Sage Accounting Standard (£43/month) and above. If you're VAT-registered you need Sage Accounting, not Sole Trader. And Sage's documentation covers UK property income, and HMRC's software finder doesn't list Sage Sole Trader & Landlord for foreign property — if you let property overseas, check with Sage before signing up. Confirm which plan you actually need before comparing headline prices with the other three tools.

Who This Is NOT For

This guide is for individual UK landlords — including landlords who also have self-employment income — who are in scope for MTD for Income Tax now or in the next phase. It is not for:

Limited companies holding property — MTD for Income Tax applies to individuals, not companies. If you hold property through a limited company, this mandate doesn't apply to you.

Partnerships — partnerships don't currently need to use MTD for Income Tax; HMRC says they'll follow at a later date. Your share of partnership profit doesn't count towards your qualifying income either.

Landlords with qualifying income of £20,000 or less — no confirmed phase covers you. Phase 3 (from 6 April 2028) applies to qualifying income over £20,000 in 2026–27; the government has said it's exploring lower thresholds, but nothing is confirmed. Remember qualifying income includes any self-employment income, not just rent.

What This Guide Doesn't Do

This page compares software features, pricing, and MTD compatibility — it does not tell you whether you personally are in scope, how to calculate your qualifying income, or how to handle a specific tax situation. Those are determinations for you or your accountant to make, not something a comparison page (or any software) should decide for you. Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need MTD software if I'm a landlord earning under £50,000?

Not from April 2026 if your qualifying income in 2024–25 was £50,000 or less — but the threshold is falling. Qualifying income is your gross self-employment and property income added together, before expenses, so count both. Phase 2 brings in qualifying income over £30,000 in 2025–26, from 6 April 2027. Phase 3 brings in qualifying income over £20,000 in 2026–27, from 6 April 2028. Starting digital records before your phase isn't required, but it removes the scramble when it begins.

Which software is best for a landlord with more than one property?

FreeAgent is the only one of the four with a dedicated property feature — its landlord account gives you a profit and loss report for each property. The other three use general-purpose features you set up yourself: tracking categories in Xero, Classes or Locations (Plus and Advanced plans) or tags (Simple Start and above — check first) in QuickBooks, and Analysis types in Sage Accounting Standard and above.

Can I use the same software for rental income and self-employment income?

Yes, with some tools. Xero says you can manage self-employment and property income in the same subscription. Sage Sole Trader's £7/month version can hold both as separate businesses under one login and one subscription; the free version allows one income source. FreeAgent and QuickBooks need two separate accounts. Whichever you choose, each income stream sends its own quarterly updates. Check the current setup with the vendor before you sign up.

Is MTD for Income Tax the same as MTD for VAT?

No. MTD for VAT became mandatory for businesses above the VAT threshold in April 2019, and for all VAT-registered businesses in April 2022. It's a separate requirement. MTD for Income Tax is the newer one: it covers sole traders' and landlords' income tax and is being phased in from 6 April 2026 by qualifying income. This guide covers MTD for Income Tax for landlords.

What happens if I don't comply once I'm in scope?

Once you're in, HMRC expects digital records, quarterly updates, and a year-end tax return sent through your software by 31 January after the tax year. Penalties under MTD for Income Tax are point-based. HMRC won't give penalties for missing a quarterly update deadline in the 2026–27 tax year, but late tax returns still get points. For the full rules, see HMRC's penalties guidance on gov.uk, or speak to an accountant.
