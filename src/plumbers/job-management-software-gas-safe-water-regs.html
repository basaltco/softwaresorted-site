Job Management Software for Gas Safe & Water Regs: Which Tools Actually Handle Compliance?

Most job management software is built around quoting, scheduling, and invoicing. Compliance documentation is an afterthought — or absent entirely. For UK plumbers registered with Gas Safe, or those carrying out work under the Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations, this matters enormously. The wrong choice means running two separate systems: your job management tool for the commercial workflow, and a second system (paper, PDF, or specialist app) for every compliance record you're legally required to produce.

This guide covers exactly what each tool does and doesn't do across the compliance requirements that affect UK plumbing businesses in 2026.

The Compliance Requirements UK Plumbers Face

Before comparing the software, it's worth being precise about what "compliance" actually means here. There are four distinct areas:

1. Gas Safe Registration Records

In England and Wales, notifiable gas work — mainly installing a heat-producing appliance such as a boiler — has to be notified to the local authority under regulation 20 of the Building Regulations 2010. As a Gas Safe registered engineer you do that through Gas Safe Register's competent person scheme within 30 days of finishing the work, and the householder then receives a Building Regulations compliance certificate. Separately, the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998 require you to be Gas Safe registered, and you must hold a current Gas Safe card for the type of work you're doing. Software can't do the notification for you, but it can record the notification reference against the job and store evidence of engineer competency.

2. Gas Safety Records (CP12 / Landlord Certificates)

Landlords are legally required to have the gas appliances and flues they provide in a rented property safety-checked every year by a Gas Safe registered engineer, under the Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998. The landlord must give a copy of the record to existing tenants within 28 days of the check and to new tenants before they move in, and keep each record for at least two years. There's no official form: HSE says Gas Safe Register doesn't produce its own gas safety check record form, and landlords can design their own. What the law fixes is the content. Under regulation 36 the record must include the date of the check; the address of the property; the name and address of the landlord or their agent; a description and the location of each appliance or flue checked; any defect found and the remedial action taken; confirmation that the check met the requirements of regulation 36(9); and the name, signature and registration number of the engineer who did it. A job note could hold all of that, but a certificate form that asks for every item, takes the signature and produces a clean PDF is far easier to get right every time — and that's where most job management software falls short. (It's commonly called a CP12; the official term is Landlord Gas Safety Record.)

3. Legionella Risk Assessments

Landlords and those responsible for commercial premises must assess and control the risk of Legionella bacteria in water systems; the Health and Safety Executive's Approved Code of Practice L8 sets out how. For residential lettings, HSE says most landlords can assess the risk themselves, no “legionella test certificate” is required, and landlords are not necessarily required to record the findings. Where a client does bring you in to assess, a written record of the system, the risk level and the control measures is usually what they're paying for. The HSE does not prescribe a specific form for it.

4. Water Regulations Compliance (Water Fittings Notifications)

In England and Wales, the Water Supply (Water Fittings) Regulations 1999 require advance notice to the water undertaker (your local water company) before certain listed work starts, and the work can't begin without its consent. The list includes, for example, a bath holding more than 230 litres, a bidet with an ascending spray or flexible hose, some shower units, a pump or booster drawing more than 12 litres a minute, a reverse osmosis unit, a reduced pressure zone (RPZ) valve or similar backflow device, a garden watering system, pipes laid outside less than 750mm or more than 1,350mm deep, a pond or pool over 10,000 litres, a new building, alterations to the water system in premises other than a house, and a material change of use. Most bathroom installations and hot water alterations aren't on it. Approved Contractors (members of a scheme such as WaterSafe) are excused advance notice for only a few of those items. When an Approved Contractor finishes the work, they must give the customer a signed certificate saying whether it complies, and send a copy to the water company if the work was notifiable.

Powered Now: Built Around UK Compliance

Powered Now is the most complete of the four tools here for UK compliance paperwork. It has a Gas Safety Record form built in — a structured certificate rather than a job note. Tradify also has UK Landlord Gas Safety Record forms, but of the four tools here only Powered Now adds a Legionella risk assessment template and native CIS (check which plan includes CIS) — Tradify has neither built in. Engineers complete the Powered Now certificate on their phone or tablet at the point of service; the record is attached to the job and can be emailed to the landlord or tenant directly from the app.

The form is a structured certificate, not a notes field, and it produces a PDF you can send to the landlord. We haven't checked it field by field against regulation 36, so complete a test record during the trial and tick off each item in the list above.

For Legionella, Powered Now includes a risk assessment template. We couldn't confirm its exact fields, so check them against how you assess during the trial. Like the gas form, it produces a PDF that can be stored against the customer record and emailed on completion.

Powered Now's compliance advantage in practice: A plumbing business doing 15 Gas Safety checks per month generates 15 records a month that need to reach landlords promptly — the landlord has 28 days from the check to give tenants a copy, and must keep each record for at least two years. With Powered Now, all of this happens within the same system as quoting and invoicing. With a tool that can't produce Gas Safety Records out of the box — Tradify on its Lite plan, ServiceM8 without a paid form, or Jobber without a checklist you've built yourself — those 15 records live in a separate system or on paper, which is a second filing system to keep on top of.

ServiceM8: Capable But Manual

ServiceM8 has a form builder that allows Gas Safe certificate templates to be created from scratch. It is genuinely capable — the platform supports conditional logic, required fields, photo attachments, and signature capture, which gives you the building blocks of a compliant Gas Safety Record. But ServiceM8 doesn't ship with a Gas Safety Record template of its own. Your options are a paid, one-off CP12 form from a third-party developer on ServiceM8's Form Store, or building one yourself and making sure it captures every item regulation 36 requires.

For a business with a developer or technically confident admin, this is achievable. For a plumbing business where the owner is also the lead engineer, building and validating a legally compliant certificate template is a significant undertaking. The same applies to Legionella — the capability is there, but the template is not.

There is a further consideration: ServiceM8 is at its best on iPhone. ServiceM8 says its Android app (ServiceM8 Lite) has almost all the same functionality as the iPhone app, but reviewers report it as weaker in practice. For a plumbing team where engineers use a mix of phones, test the full forms workflow on the Android phones during the free trial before relying on it for compliance paperwork.

On CIS, ServiceM8 supports the domestic reverse charge through a one-time setup — 0% reverse charge tax rates and a separate invoice template — but doesn't document CIS deductions. If you work as a subcontractor on commercial projects or new-build sites, check how your accountant will handle the deductions.

Tradify: Gas Certs Yes, No Legionella Template or Native CIS

Tradify has UK Gas Safety Record forms on its Pro and Plus plans, including Landlord/Homeowner Gas Safety Records, Boiler Records, Installation/Commissioning/Decommissioning Records, and Gas Warning Notices.

The gap that matters for many Gas Safe plumbers: no Legionella risk assessment template (its checklist forms may stretch to one — check during the trial), and no native CIS option — you need Xero or Sage. For a plumber who carries out Legionella assessments alongside boiler servicing, Tradify's compliance coverage remains partial. For a plumber who only needs Gas Safety Records — boiler services, annual landlord checks, no commercial water system work — Tradify's gas forms are worth evaluating alongside Powered Now's.

For plumbers who do not do gas work — water-only, drainage, bathroom installation — Tradify remains a strong choice for its quoting workflow and Xero integration. Water Regulations notices go to your local water company regardless of which tool you use.

Jobber: Not Designed for UK Compliance

Jobber is a Canadian product. It handles the core job management workflow well — quoting, scheduling, invoicing, client communication — but it has no UK compliance templates. There's no Gas Safety Record, Legionella or Water Regulations form built in, and no documented CIS support. Its custom checklists can take a signature, produce a PDF and be saved offline, so you could build your own forms, but you'd be designing and checking them yourself. It is priced in team-size bands rather than per user, and on Connect, the plan we compare teams on, it works out cheaper than per-user tools from about four staff, but that advantage does not outweigh the compliance gap for any Gas Safe registered plumbing business.

Jobber's strongest use case in UK plumbing is a larger water-only or drainage business — three or more field staff — where the main compliance paperwork is Water Regulations notices to the local water company and certificates for customers, and the primary challenge is managing team schedules, client communication, and cash flow. For that use case, Jobber is worth serious consideration. For any business doing gas work, it is not the right tool.

What "Custom Forms" Actually Means

ServiceM8 and Tradify's responses to compliance gaps often involve suggesting that users build custom forms. This deserves scrutiny. A custom form is not the same as a compliant certificate. For Gas Safety Records specifically, regulation 36 sets out what the record must contain, including the engineer's signature, but not what it has to look like. A form that captures all of it covers the legal content. The hard part is making sure no item can be skipped and that the PDF it produces is clear, with your company name and the engineer's Gas Safe number on it. Building that in a form builder, validating it against the regulatory requirements, and maintaining it as requirements change is a meaningful ongoing task.

With Powered Now, the gas form comes ready-made. That saves the build, though it's still worth checking it against the regulation 36 list during the trial. For most plumbing businesses, a ready-made form is worth more than the flexibility of building from scratch.

Offline Working and Compliance

Gas safety and Legionella work often happens in locations with poor mobile signal: commercial basements, roof plant rooms, remote rural properties. This creates a practical problem for any compliance workflow that depends on cloud connectivity. Powered Now's mobile app works offline — the certificate can be completed without a signal, and it syncs when connectivity returns. Tradify requires signal. ServiceM8 lets you view and update synced jobs offline, but its Forms — including a CP12 form — can't be finalised until you have signal. Jobber has added an offline mode.

For a plumber doing annual boiler services on a rural estate with patchy signal, offline working is not a nice-to-have. A certificate that cannot be completed on-site must be completed later from notes — introducing error risk and delaying the record reaching the landlord, who has 28 days from the date of the check to give existing tenants a copy.
