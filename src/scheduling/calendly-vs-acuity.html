Calendly vs Acuity Scheduling: Which Is Right for Your UK Business? (2026)

Plenty of the "Calendly vs Acuity" comparisons online are published by rival tools — Zeeg, Koalendar, and Cal.com each have a commercial reason to steer you a particular way. This comparison has no affiliate relationship with either tool, so the recommendation depends entirely on what your business actually needs: simplicity, or customisation.

Quick Verdict

Choose Calendly if you offer one type of appointment and want the fastest possible setup with a usable free tier. Choose Acuity Scheduling if you offer multiple services, sell packages or memberships, or need detailed custom intake forms — Acuity has no free plan, just a 7-day trial, but the configuration depth justifies the extra setup time for a more complex booking need.

Head-to-Head by Use Case

  • Solo freelancer, one type of call or meeting → Calendly. Faster setup, free tier covers it.
  • Therapist or coach selling session packages → Acuity. Native package sales beat Calendly's single-event model.
  • Salon or clinic with multiple treatment types and durations → Acuity. Better suited to varied appointment configuration.
  • Want to try before paying anything → Calendly. Acuity has only a 7-day trial.

What It Costs in Practice

The two price in different ways. Calendly charges per seat — per person who takes bookings — and bills new UK customers in pounds. Acuity charges per account, with a limit on how many calendars you can run, and bills only in US dollars. That difference decides which is cheaper once there's more than one of you.

On your own, Calendly is cheaper at every stage: free for a single type of call, and £8 a month against Acuity Starter's $16 (about £12 at £1 ≈ $1.34, checked 6 October 2026) once you need more. But the plans aren't like for like — Acuity Starter already includes package and membership sales, which Calendly doesn't offer natively at any price, plus custom intake forms. And as soon as several people take bookings, the per-seat model turns against Calendly: four practitioners cost £32 a month on Calendly Standard, against $27 (about £20) for Acuity Standard covering up to six calendars. Remember Acuity bills in dollars, so its sterling cost moves with the exchange rate.

Setting Up the Same Service in Each

Take a personal trainer offering a free 15-minute consultation and paid one-hour sessions, sold individually or as a block of ten. In Calendly, the consultation and the session are two event types, so you need the Standard plan rather than Free. You connect Stripe or PayPal to charge for each session as it's booked. What Calendly can't do natively is sell the block of ten: you'd sell that some other way and then send those clients a booking link.

In Acuity, the consultation and the session are two appointment types with their own lengths and buffer times, and the block of ten is set up as a package that clients buy once and book against. You can add an intake form that the client fills in before their first session — health questions, goals, injuries — and those details sit with the booking. It takes longer to configure than Calendly, but it's all available on the Starter plan.

The Catches, Side by Side

Calendly's catch: Check UK time zone display — test your booking flow before relying on it for client-facing meetings. The free tier is limited to one event type.

Acuity's catch: No free plan and only a 7-day trial, and the setup for multiple appointment types and intake forms takes longer than Calendly's. Expect a steeper start.

GDPR Note

Neither has a GDPR edge over the other. Both hold data in the US and rely on the Data Privacy Framework for the transfer, and both build their Data Processing Addendum into the terms you accept at sign-up. Acuity's UK customers contract with Squarespace Ireland rather than a US company, but the data may still go to Squarespace's US servers. See our GDPR guide.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which is cheaper, Calendly or Acuity Scheduling?

Calendly has a usable free plan, and its Standard plan is £8 per seat a month billed annually, against Acuity's Starter at $16 a month billed annually. Acuity has no free plan — only a 7-day trial — so you commit to a paid plan sooner.

Which has a free plan, Calendly or Acuity?

Calendly does, with one event type and one calendar connection. Acuity offers a 7-day free trial only.

Which is better for selling packages or memberships?

Acuity Scheduling. Native package and membership sales with payment integration is one of its core strengths, and not something Calendly offers natively.

Which is easier to set up?

Calendly — a single event type can be live in around 15 minutes. Acuity's value comes from configuring multiple appointment types and intake forms, which takes longer.

Which is more GDPR-friendly for UK businesses?

Neither has a clear advantage. Both hold data in the US under the Data Privacy Framework, and both include their DPA in the terms you accept. Read the DPA either way, especially if you'll collect sensitive information.
