GDPR-Compliant Scheduling Software for UK Businesses (2026)

Sooner or later a client asks where their booking details end up. For most scheduling tools the honest answer is "on servers in another country, under a legal agreement you accepted when you signed up". That's usually fine — but it's worth knowing exactly what you agreed to, and it matters a lot more if your booking form asks about someone's health.

Quick Verdict

All four tools here can be used lawfully by a UK business, and three of them — Calendly, Acuity Scheduling and YouCanBook.me — build their Data Processing Addendum (DPA) into the terms you accept at sign-up, so there's nothing separate to sign. The real differences are where your clients' data is held and which legal route covers getting it there. Calendly, Acuity and YouCanBook.me hold data in the US. SimplyBook.me is an EU company (based in Cyprus), but its data centres are in Canada, France and Singapore, and its promise to keep data in the EU is made to EU-based businesses — so a UK business should check where its own account's data sits. None of the four is the obvious GDPR winner; what matters is that you've read the DPA and thought about what your booking form collects.

What "GDPR Compliant" Actually Means Here

A scheduling tool being "GDPR compliant" doesn't mean it's fine for any kind of data. It means the vendor has the legal pieces in place to process personal data lawfully on your behalf. You're still the one collecting the data, so you still decide what's appropriate to ask for and which tool to trust with it.

Three things matter for this comparison:

The Data Processing Addendum (DPA). The contract setting out how the vendor handles your clients' data on your behalf. Calendly says its DPA is incorporated into its Terms of Use — "There is nothing additional for you to sign". Squarespace (which runs Acuity) says its DPA "forms part of" its Terms of Service, and YouCanBook.me says its DPA "forms part of our customer agreement". SimplyBook.me publishes a DPA overview and a signed version. Calendly and YouCanBook.me will also provide a signed copy on request, which is useful for your records.

Where the data is held. Calendly is a US company. Acuity's UK and European customers contract with Squarespace Ireland Limited, but Squarespace says data may be transferred "to our servers in the US". YouCanBook.me says its infrastructure "is hosted in the United States (AWS)". SimplyBook.me stores data in data centres in Canada, France and Singapore.

The legal route for international transfers. Moving UK personal data to the US needs a legal basis. Calendly and Squarespace rely mainly on the Data Privacy Framework, with Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) also built into Calendly's DPA. YouCanBook.me uses SCCs plus the UK International Data Transfer Addendum.

Who Should Care Most About This

This matters more for some businesses than others. If you're a freelance graphic designer booking discovery calls, the data you collect through a booking page (name, email, project type) is low-sensitivity, and any of the four tools is a reasonable choice.

If you're a therapist, counsellor, medical-adjacent practitioner, or any business collecting health, financial, or otherwise special-category data at the point of booking, the ICO treats this as higher-risk processing. In that case:

  • Read the vendor's DPA in full before collecting any client data through the tool, not after.
  • Ask yourself whether the booking form needs the sensitive detail at all. Often the booking only needs a name, a time and a contact number, and the rest can be collected later in a more appropriate way.
  • If where the data is held matters to you, check it for your own account rather than going by where the company is based — the two aren't the same for any of these tools.

What to Actually Do Before You Commit

  1. Find the vendor's DPA (linked from its privacy or legal pages) and read what it covers. For Calendly, Acuity and YouCanBook.me it's already part of the terms you accept; ask for a signed copy if you want one for your records.
  2. Check where your data will be held. For SimplyBook.me, confirm what applies to a UK account.
  3. If you're collecting anything beyond basic contact and scheduling information, write down why you're satisfied the tool meets your obligations — good practice even if no one ever asks.
  4. Re-check once a year, or after the vendor changes hands (as YouCanBook.me did in 2025) — terms and hosting can change without much announcement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a Data Processing Addendum (DPA) to use Calendly in the UK?

You need one in place, and with Calendly you already have it. Calendly says its DPA is incorporated into its Terms of Use: "There is nothing additional for you to sign or execute, and by accepting the Terms of Use, the DPA is already in place for you." It includes the UK Addendum, and Calendly will provide a signed copy on request.

Which scheduling tool stores data in the UK or EEA?

None of the four guarantees UK storage on its standard plans. Calendly, Acuity and YouCanBook.me hold data in the US. SimplyBook.me's data centres are in Canada, France and Singapore; it says data for EU-based businesses is stored in the EU, and its enterprise clients can choose dedicated servers, including in the UK. If UK or EEA storage is a requirement, confirm it for your own account before you sign up.

What's the difference between GDPR compliance and data residency?

GDPR compliance means the legal mechanisms (a DPA and a lawful route for any international transfer) are in place to process data properly, wherever it's held. Data residency is about where the data is physically stored and processed. A tool can be GDPR compliant without UK or EEA data residency, as long as the transfer route is in place.

Do I need extra safeguards if I'm a therapist or healthcare-adjacent business?

Yes. The ICO treats health and similar special-category data as higher-risk processing. Read the vendor's DPA in full, check where the data will be held, and think about whether your booking form needs to collect sensitive details at all.

What are Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) and the Data Privacy Framework?

Both are legal routes for sending personal data from the UK or EU to a country such as the US. SCCs are standard contract terms built into the vendor's agreement; the Data Privacy Framework is a scheme US companies can sign up to. Calendly and Squarespace (Acuity) rely mainly on the Data Privacy Framework, and Calendly's DPA also includes SCCs. YouCanBook.me uses SCCs with the UK's International Data Transfer Addendum.

Which scheduling tool is the safest default for GDPR-sensitive use cases?

There isn't a clear winner. The three US-hosted tools all have their DPA built into their terms and a stated transfer route; SimplyBook.me is an EU company but doesn't hold all data in the EU. For sensitive work, what makes the biggest difference is reading the DPA, checking where your account's data sits, and keeping sensitive questions off the booking form where you can.
